# How to open a Litecoin wallet

To open a Litecoin wallet, use Nexus Wallet, Cake Wallet, or Electrum-LTC. Those are the wallets listed for storing Litecoin. The page that lists them is https://litecoin.com/store. This article does not rank the three. A hardware device is a separate choice and is not one of these apps.

Nexus Wallet is the Litecoin Foundation's mobile app. It is self-custody: the keys stay on the phone, and the phone signs each payment. It can send ordinary Litecoin, and it can make a private Litecoin payment that hides the amount and the addresses. That private payment is MWEB, and it is opt-in. A normal payment that never enters MWEB still shows the amount and the addresses. Buy and sell controls inside the app appear only where they are legally available.

Cake Wallet is a free, open-source, non-custodial mobile wallet. It can store, exchange, and spend Litecoin along with other coins. The keys stay on the device. It is a second phone wallet beside Nexus Wallet.

Electrum-LTC is a desktop wallet. It does not download the whole chain. It checks payments against its servers, and the secret phrase and private keys stay on the computer. You hold the keys, and you can export them to another Litecoin wallet that accepts them. It is a community port of the Electrum wallet for Litecoin, not a product of Electrum Technologies.

For a private MWEB payment, open one of these wallets and use a wallet that can pay to an MWEB address. Those addresses start with ltcmweb1. Nexus Wallet is the Foundation app that turns on that private payment. Cake Wallet and Electrum-LTC are the other two wallets to open for Litecoin itself.

Whichever you open, the recovery phrase recreates the keys. Write it on paper or metal and store it offline. Do not type it into a website, a chat, or a support form, including this one. Anyone who has the phrase can move the LTC. The Litecoin Foundation cannot restore a lost phrase and cannot freeze the coins. Opening a wallet this way is self-custody. It is different from leaving LTC on an exchange, where the exchange holds the keys.
