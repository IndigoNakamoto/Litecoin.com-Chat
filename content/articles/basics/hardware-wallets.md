# Hardware wallets for Litecoin

Yes. You can store Litecoin on a hardware wallet. A hardware wallet is a small device whose job is to hold the private keys that spend LTC and to sign transactions inside the device. The keys are not supposed to leave it. A computer or phone builds the unsigned payment and shows the address and amount; the device signs only after the holder confirms the same address and amount on the device's own screen. That check matters because a compromised computer can display one address while asking the device to pay another.

Ledger and Trezor are two widely used examples of hardware wallets that can hold Litecoin, alongside other coins. Naming them is not a recommendation from the Litecoin Foundation, and this article does not rank firmware, vendors, or resellers. Buy from the manufacturer's own shop, not from a marketplace listing or a message that offers a "pre-seeded" device. A device that arrives with a recovery phrase already written down is not safe to use.

The recovery phrase (often 12 or 24 words) can recreate the keys. It should be written on paper or metal and stored offline. Do not type it into a website, a chat, or a support form, including this one. Anyone who has the phrase can move the LTC. The Foundation cannot restore a lost phrase and cannot freeze the coins. A hardware wallet also does not make a payment reversible. Once a signed transaction is confirmed on Litecoin, it is as final as a payment signed by any other wallet.

A hardware wallet is a form of self-custody. It is different from leaving LTC on an exchange, where the exchange holds the keys. It is also different from a software wallet on a phone, which keeps keys on a general-purpose computer. The device still needs a Litecoin app or account in its firmware that understands Litecoin addresses (L…, M…, and ltc1…). If a model does not list Litecoin, do not assume a Bitcoin account will spend LTC.
