# Litecoin supply and the halving

The maximum supply of Litecoin is 84 million LTC. That cap is in the consensus rules, not a policy the Litecoin Foundation can vote to raise. New coins enter circulation only as the block subsidy paid to the miner who publishes a valid block. Transaction fees are separate: they move existing coins from the sender to the miner and do not increase the supply.

## What is the Litecoin halving?

The halving is the scheduled cut in the block subsidy. Litecoin starts at 50 LTC per block. Every 840,000 blocks the subsidy is cut in half: 50, then 25, then 12.5, then 6.25, and so on. At a 2.5-minute target, 840,000 blocks is about four years. The third halving, at block 2,520,000, landed in August 2023. The subsidy keeps halving until it rounds down to zero, which is how the chain approaches the 84 million cap instead of minting forever.

The halving changes the issuance rate. It does not change the coins already in existence, and it does not guarantee a price move. Miners still receive whatever transaction fees the block contains. After many halvings the subsidy becomes a small part of a miner's revenue and fees matter more. Nothing in the halving pays a holder for leaving coins untouched. The subsidy goes to the miner of that block.

People sometimes say "the stock" or "how many Litecoin exist" when they mean two different numbers. The maximum supply is 84 million. The circulating supply is the subsidy issued so far, minus coins that are lost or unmoved, and it is always lower than the cap until issuance ends. A live circulating figure, if one is shown, comes from chain data at that moment, not from this article.
